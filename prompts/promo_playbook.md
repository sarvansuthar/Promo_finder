# Promo playbook

You are choosing moments that already exist in the footage. You cannot add voiceover, text,
music or new lines, and you cannot reorder a scene. Find beats that do these jobs on their own.

## 1. The hook: the first 3 seconds

The strongest openings stack three jobs into the first beats:
- 0–1 s: an arresting visual or action, such as a slap, a wound, chains, a wedding interrupted,
  or someone kneeling.
- 1–2 s: a provocative line the viewer has to process.
- 2–3 s: the stakes, or an open question.

A clip that opens on calm setup, a greeting or a scenic shot has already lost the scroller.

Label each promo with its main hook type:
- `shock_action`: sudden violence or physical humiliation (slap, strike, shove, thrown into mud).
- `reveal`: a secret, an identity or a betrayal exposed.
- `contradiction`: something that should be impossible ("the dead man just called me").
- `threat`: danger with a deadline or an ultimatum ("do this, or he hangs").
- `injustice`: an innocent person punished or humiliated in public.
- `curiosity_gap`: incomplete but meaningful information that demands the missing piece.
- `emotional_conflict`: love against loyalty, a sacrifice, a choice with a cost.
- `pattern_interrupt`: a moment that breaks expectations (the beggar who fights like a soldier,
  the jilted bride who refuses to cry).

## 2. The shape of a 20–60 s promo

The promo runs in four steps:
1. Hook (0–3 s).
2. Just enough context to know who is wronging whom.
3. Escalation, as the stakes rise.
4. Cliffhanger.

If the middle drags, use a second segment to jump over it. Never use a second segment to join
unrelated scenes.

## 3. The cliffhanger: cut before the payoff

- **Interrupted moments stick (the Zeigarnik effect).** Cut while the door is opening, the hand
  is raised, the question is asked, or the proposal awaits an answer. Never cut on the answer.
- **Information gap.** The viewer understands the situation but is denied the one thing they
  most want to know.
- **Loss aversion.** By the end it must be clear what someone is about to *lose*: their life,
  their love, their name or their home.

Cliffhanger types: `question_unanswered`, `threat_imminent`, `blow_about_to_land`,
`reveal_interrupted`, `arrival`, `decision_pending`, `reaction_hold`.

These are bad endings: a hug, a relieved smile, a threat already dealt with, a question already
answered, or a scene that simply winds down.

## 4. Standalone: good mystery, not confusion

Good mystery creates questions; confusion creates frustration. A stranger must understand from
the clip alone who the victim is, who is wronging them, and what is at stake.

Mark a clip down when it relies on:
- names that are never explained;
- objects whose meaning comes from earlier episodes;
- inside references ("after what you did last week").

Relationship words in the dialogue (sister, husband, bride, stepmother, heir) are what make a
clip readable to a cold viewer.

## 5. Ranking for posting

Post first the promos that sell the show's premise in one clip. Then post the strongest
hook-plus-cliffhanger pairs. Vary hook types across the first posts so the feed doesn't repeat
the same beat. Two clips that tell the same story beat are duplicates, even from different
footage.
